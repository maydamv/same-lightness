      Under the hood

      A pixel is three lights. HSL lightness is (strongest + weakest) / 2, so every pure colour is 50%. Our eyes weigh them: green 72%, red 21%, blue 7%. I undo the screen's gamma, multiply by the weights and add: yellow is 0.21 + 0.72 = 0.93.

      HSL's lightness is only the middle point between the strongest and the weakest channel. It doesn't know that our eyes are much more sensitive to green than to blue. Relative luminance does.

      And OKLCH?

      OKLCH models the eye: three cones, then a cube root, because the eye compresses light. Check: at L 60%, 0.6³ ≈ 0.22, right where the OKLCH greys are. Evil Martians: you can change the hue without breaking contrast. Out-of-screen colours: I lower the chroma instead of clipping.

      OKLCH is calculated in a different way: it starts from a model of the eye, so its L is the lightness you perceive.

      1. Undo the screen's gamma curve, like luminance does.
      2. Turn red, green and blue into the response of the eye's three kinds of cone, with a 3×3 matrix.
      3. Take the cube root of each one. The eye compresses light: twice the light doesn't look twice as light.
      4. Mix them again into L, a and b. L is the perceived lightness; chroma and hue are a and b written as a distance and an angle.

      Why designers are moving to it

      • The same L looks equally light in every hue, so you can change a colour's hue without breaking its contrast.
      • Making a colour 10% lighter gives the same result on every hue. In HSL, blue and purple react differently.
      • It can describe the extra P3 colours of modern screens, which rgb(), hex and hsl() can't.
      • You can read it: oklch(70% 0.12 240) is a medium-light, soft blue.

      Can you read it?

      Click both buttons. Same HSL lightness, white text: 1.07 on yellow, 8.59 on blue. Pick colours by HSL and you can ship text nobody can read.

      Text needs enough difference in light from its background. WCAG measures it with the same luminance: contrast = (lighter + 0.05) / (darker + 0.05).
